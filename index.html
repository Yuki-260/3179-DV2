Museums in Australia: preserving history, reaching today's audience

17.2% of Australians aged 15+ visited a museum in 2021–22. Almost six in ten went only once.

This page asks who museums reach, where they are, and how visits are changing. It uses ABS survey data and six years of Sydney visit counts.

Preservation and engagement

The data show who comes, not what is on display. Three things stand out.

Repeat visits are rare, so new exhibitions matter. Access is uneven by income and education. And change is possible: the Australian Museum nearly quadrupled its visits since 2018–19.

Preserving the past and reaching today's audience are not opposites. Neither happens by itself.

About the data

  • Visit counts cover six NSW state institutions, 2018–19 to 2023–24. No national series exists.
  • Survey figures are ABS estimates for people aged 15+ in 2021–22. Small groups carry larger margins of error.
  • The data measure who visits, not what is on display. No exhibition or collection data was available.
  • The treemap sums rates that overlap, because people attend more than one venue.
